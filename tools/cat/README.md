# The shelf cat

The cat that wanders along the bottom of the homepage is drawn from silhouette sprite sheets
(`images/cat-*.png`), and `cat.js` plays them. Both are generated by the scripts here from
seven short videos of a black cat on a white background, made with a video model.

```sh
tools/cat/build.sh <videos dir> [work dir]
```

It takes about ten minutes and needs Node and an `ffmpeg` with the `minterpolate` filter.
It overwrites the sheets and `cat.js`, and moves the cache version in `index.html` on. With
`OUT=some/dir` set, it writes the sheets and `cat.js` there instead, to compare a rebuild with
what's in the repository.

## The videos

The videos dir holds these, all 736x400 at 24fps, side-on, the cat facing left:

| File | What happens | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| `walk.mp4` | walks steadily, the camera following | `walk`, one stride looped |
| `startstop.mp4` | stands, sets off, walks, stops | `start`, `stop` |
| `idle.mp4` | stands still, swishing its tail | `idle`, looped |
| `sit.mp4` | sits down, looks around, stands up | `sitdown`, `sitloop`, `standup` |
| `jump.mp4` | crouches, leaps, lands, settles | `jump` |
| `turn.mp4` | turns round to face right | `turn` |
| `pet.mp4` | arches its back into an unseen hand | `petin`, `petloop`, `petout` |

The videos aren't in the repository. The prompts that made them asked for a locked-off camera,
a strict side view, a pure white background with no floor or shadow, and a realistic black cat:
video models animate a real cat far better than a flat shape, and a black cat on white keys
cleanly into a silhouette.

## How the sheets are made

1. **Silhouettes** (`silhouettes.js`): the dark pixels, keeping the cat's own blob and filling
   its eyes and the sheen on its fur.
2. **The walk** (`walk-frames.js`, `loop-morph.js`, `fix-head.js`, `walk-travel.js`): frames
   40–140 of the walking video, motion-interpolated to 96fps; one stride starting at frame 119,
   22.75 video frames long, whose last third morphs into the stride before it so the loop has no
   seam; one ear shape and a smooth neck line in every exposure, because the video redraws the
   near ear differently each frame; and a travel table halfway between keeping the paws planted
   and moving the body evenly.
3. **One cell for everything** (`place.js`): every clip scaled to the walk's cat and placed in
   a 768x480 working cell (256x160 in the sheets) with the ground on one row.
4. **Joins** (`join.js`, `turn.js`, `pet.js`): each clip starts and ends on a pose the clip it
   hands over to also has. Start and stop meet the walk on matching exposures; clips that begin or
   end standing meet the idle on whichever exposure looks most like them; the turn lands on the
   idle mirrored. What's left is covered by a morph (`lib.js`) that finds a smooth deformation
   carrying one silhouette onto the other, so tails, ears and legs move instead of fading.
5. **Sheets and travel** (`sheets.js`): edges smoothed, scaled to 256x160 cells, 16 to a row;
   travel tables for the clips that move the cat (starting and stopping ease into the walk's
   speed; the jump, filmed with a following camera, gets its forward leap back).
6. **Bridges** (`bridges.js`): from every quarter second of standing and sitting, a 0.4s morph
   into the pose petting starts from or into standing up, so petting answers quickly.
7. **Clean up** (`clean.js`): specks wiped, and any bridge that still comes apart listed so the
   cat waits past it.
8. **cat.js** (`cat-js.js`): `cat.template.js` with the clip table filled in.

The frame ranges, scales and cut points in these scripts were chosen for these particular
videos; new videos will need them looked at again.

## Checks

```sh
node tools/cat/check/simulate.js cat.js 600 7 > sim.txt          # ten minutes against a simulated clock
node tools/cat/check/switches.js sim.txt <work dir> -             # any frame that jumps, especially at a switch
node tools/cat/check/fragments.js <work dir> walk idle jump ...   # any silhouette in pieces
```

`simulate.js` runs the real `cat.js` against a stand-in page, taking a list of frames to tap
on and, in `STROKES`, frames to stroke on.
